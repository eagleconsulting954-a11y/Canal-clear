VUMPA Filing

What is VUMPA for the Panama Canal?

VUMPA (Vessel Universal Measurement and Pre-Arrival) is the Panama Canal Authority's mandatory pre-arrival notification and documentation system required for all canal transits.

VUMPA consolidates vessel particulars, crew data, equipment certifications, PCSOPEP environmental plans, and cargo declarations into a single submission. The ACP uses VUMPA data to assign transit slots, calculate tolls, plan pilot assignments, and conduct pre-arrival compliance checks. Every vessel — regardless of size, type, or flag — must submit VUMPA before transiting the Panama Canal.

How do I file VUMPA for Panama Canal transit?

File VUMPA through the ACP Maritime Service Portal at least 96 hours before your scheduled Panama Canal arrival, following these 7 steps.

Step 1: Register or log in to the ACP Maritime Service Portal with valid agent or operator credentials.
Step 2: Create a new transit application and select your vessel from registered profiles.
Step 3: Enter or confirm vessel particulars — dimensions, tonnage, draft, IMO number, call sign.
Step 4: Submit the crew manifest with all credentials and STCW certificate numbers.
Step 5: Attach PCSOPEP document and required vessel safety certificates.
Step 6: Enter cargo declaration with commodity codes and hazmat classifications if applicable.
Step 7: Submit and monitor for ACP acceptance confirmation.

CanalClear automates all 7 steps and pre-validates before submission to eliminate rejections.

How far in advance must VUMPA be filed?

VUMPA must be filed at least 96 hours (4 days) before your scheduled arrival at Balboa anchorage (Pacific) or Cristóbal anchorage (Atlantic).

The 96-hour deadline is a hard cutoff set by the ACP. Submissions received after the window result in automatic transit slot forfeiture — there is no grace period. The ACP's system performs machine validation at submission; rejected packages that cannot be corrected and resubmitted within the window also result in slot loss. CanalClear tracks deadlines per vessel and sends alerts at 120 hours, 96 hours, and 72 hours before arrival.

PCSOPEP Documentation

What is PCSOPEP?

PCSOPEP (Panama Canal Shipboard Oil Pollution Emergency Plan) is a mandatory environmental compliance document required by the Panama Canal Authority for all transiting vessels.

PCSOPEP documents the vessel's specific procedures for responding to oil spills or pollution incidents during canal transit. It must be vessel-specific (not generic), current with the latest ACP-issued requirements, and submitted as part of every VUMPA pre-arrival package. The ACP reviews PCSOPEP version numbers and may reject filings with outdated or non-compliant plans. CanalClear validates your PCSOPEP against current ACP version requirements before VUMPA submission.

Is PCSOPEP the same as SOPEP?

No — PCSOPEP is a Panama Canal-specific document that supplements (and is distinct from) the MARPOL SOPEP requirement.

SOPEP (Shipboard Oil Pollution Emergency Plan) is the global MARPOL Annex I requirement for oil tankers over 150 GT and all other ships over 400 GT. PCSOPEP adds Panama Canal-specific procedures on top of SOPEP — different reporting contacts, specific canal zone spill response protocols, and ACP-format documentation requirements. Vessels transiting Panama Canal need both a valid SOPEP and a current ACP-compliant PCSOPEP.

How often must PCSOPEP be updated?

PCSOPEP must be updated whenever ACP requirements change, vessel specifications change, or at minimum during every scheduled dry-dock survey.

The ACP periodically revises PCSOPEP requirements. Each revision requires vessel operators to update their PCSOPEP to the current ACP template version. Vessels with outdated PCSOPEP versions are rejected at VUMPA validation. CanalClear monitors ACP PCSOPEP version updates and notifies fleet operators when their documents require updating.

Transit Documents & Requirements

What documents are required for Panama Canal transit?

Panama Canal transit requires 6 document categories: VUMPA pre-arrival package, PCSOPEP, crew manifest with STCW credentials, cargo declaration, vessel certificates, and ACP measurement data.

1. VUMPA pre-arrival package — Filed 96+ hours before arrival via ACP portal
2. PCSOPEP — Current Panama Canal Shipboard Oil Pollution Emergency Plan
3. Crew manifest — All crew with valid STCW certificates, passports, and seafarer ID documents
4. Cargo declaration — Cargo type, quantity, origin, destination, hazmat codes
5. Vessel certificates — Classification, international tonnage, load line, safety equipment, safety construction
6. ACP measurement data — Current Panama Canal measurement certificate (PC/UMS tonnage)

CanalClear organizes and validates all 6 categories, checking expiry dates and ACP compliance requirements for each.

What crew credentials are required for Panama Canal transit?

All crew must have valid STCW certificates appropriate to their rank, valid passports, and valid seafarer's identity documents (SID or STCW).

Key credentials checked in VUMPA crew manifest:
• STCW Basic Safety Training (all crew)
• Officer of the Watch certificate (deck officers)
• Chief Engineer / Engineer Officer STCW certificates
• Medical certificates (valid, not expired)
• Passport validity (minimum 6 months past transit date in most flag states)
• Seafarer's Identity Document or STCW Certificate of Competency

CanalClear validates all credential expiry dates against your transit date and flags any expiring within 60 days.

What cargo declarations are required for Panama Canal transit?

Cargo declarations for Panama Canal transit must include cargo type, quantity, weight, origin, destination, and IMO hazmat classifications if applicable.

For general cargo: commodity description, quantity, total weight, port of loading, port of discharge, Bill of Lading references.
For hazardous materials: IMO hazard class and division, UN number, packaging group, and applicable emergency procedures (per IMDG Code).
For bulk liquids (tankers): cargo grade, flash point, density, loading port, discharge port, and quantity in metric tons and barrels.
Cargo declarations are submitted within the VUMPA package and cannot be amended after the 96-hour window.

ACP Maritime Service Portal

What is the ACP Maritime Service Portal?

The ACP Maritime Service Portal is the official online platform of the Panama Canal Authority where all VUMPA filings, transit fee payments, and canal services are processed.

All VUMPA submissions must go through the ACP Maritime Service Portal — paper and fax submissions were eliminated entirely in 2025. The portal performs automated machine validation of all VUMPA packages at submission and returns immediate acceptance or rejection status. CanalClear integrates directly with the ACP portal via API, enabling seamless VUMPA submission from within the CanalClear dashboard.

What is the Panama Canal Authority (ACP)?

The Panama Canal Authority (Autoridad del Canal de Panamá, or ACP) is the autonomous Panamanian government agency that operates and regulates the Panama Canal.

The ACP sets all transit requirements, maintains the canal infrastructure, assigns pilot services, calculates and collects tolls, and enforces compliance regulations. It was established in 1999 when Panama assumed full control of the canal from the United States. The ACP's regulatory authority extends to all vessels transiting the canal, regardless of flag state or nationality. Transit Operations

How long does a Panama Canal transit take?

A full Panama Canal transit takes 8–10 hours once underway, but vessels typically wait 12–24+ hours at anchorage before their assigned transit slot.

The canal route runs 80 km (50 miles) from Atlantic to Pacific or vice versa, passing through three lock complexes. Neo-Panamax vessels use the Agua Clara locks (Atlantic) and Cocoli locks (Pacific) opened in 2016. Panamax vessels use the original Gatun and Miraflores locks. Total time from anchorage to clear the canal typically runs 24–36 hours. VUMPA compliance determines whether you transit on your reserved slot or wait for reassignment.

How do I get a Panama Canal transit slot?

Transit slots are reserved through the ACP reservation system — typically weeks in advance for Neo-Panamax vessels, with VUMPA acceptance required to confirm the slot.

Neo-Panamax vessels book slots via the ACP's auction system or the super-preference queue. Regular slots are available first-come, first-served. Slot reservation must be made before the VUMPA filing window opens. Critically: your transit slot is only confirmed once the ACP accepts your VUMPA submission. A VUMPA rejection or late filing voids your slot reservation. CanalClear's early-submission approach ensures VUMPA is accepted well before the slot confirmation deadline.

What is the Panama Canal pre-arrival inspection?

ACP inspectors may board vessels at anchor to verify that VUMPA documentation matches actual on-board conditions — crew, equipment, cargo, and certifications.

Pre-arrival inspections check: crew credential originals match VUMPA manifest data; PCSOPEP document is aboard, current, and crew-familiar; equipment certificates match vessel specifications; cargo condition matches cargo declaration. Discrepancies result in transit delay, ACP fines, or slot forfeiture. CanalClear's documentation accuracy validation helps ensure VUMPA data precisely reflects actual vessel conditions.

What is MARPOL and does it apply to Panama Canal transits?

MARPOL is the global convention preventing maritime pollution — Panama Canal regulations build on MARPOL standards with additional ACP-specific requirements.

MARPOL (International Convention for the Prevention of Pollution from Ships) covers six types of maritime pollution across six annexes. Key MARPOL requirements for Panama Canal transit:
• MARPOL Annex I (oil pollution): basis for PCSOPEP requirement
• MARPOL Annex II (noxious liquid substances): applies to chemical tankers
• MARPOL Annex VI (air emissions): SOx and NOx limits enforced in canal zone
• Oil Record Book: must be current and available for inspection
CanalClear validates MARPOL-related certificate currency as part of VUMPA pre-submission checks.

What is Port State Control and does it affect Panama Canal transit?

Port State Control (PSC) is the inspection regime where flag-state authorities inspect foreign vessels — Panama Canal Authority operates its own inspection program aligned with PSC standards.

PSC deficiencies on a vessel's inspection record can affect Panama Canal transit — vessels with outstanding deficiencies may face additional ACP scrutiny. The ACP references PSC inspection records in its pre-arrival risk assessment. Maintaining a clean PSC record (zero outstanding deficiencies) reduces the likelihood of ACP-initiated delays.
