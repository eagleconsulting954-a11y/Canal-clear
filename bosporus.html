What is SP-1 filing? SP-1 is the mandatory pre-arrival notification form required by Turkish maritime authorities for all vessels transiting the Bosporus or Dardanelles. It captures vessel particulars, cargo details, dangerous goods declarations, ETA, and Montreux Convention eligibility. Standard vessels must file 24 hours before transit; dangerous cargo vessels must file 48 hours in advance. Failure to file results in transit denial.

What documents do I need for Bosporus transit? Bosporus transit requires: SP-1 pre-arrival notification (24h or 48h for dangerous cargo), crew list, cargo declaration, ballast water management declaration, and Montreux Convention compliance certification for special vessel categories. The Problem

The Turkish Straits filing maze — and what it costs when you get it wrong

Every Bosporus transit touches three separate Turkish authorities, four filing deadlines, and Montreux Convention restrictions that most operators only discover at the last minute.

48h

Dangerous cargo advance notice

IMDG-classified cargo requires a 48-hour pre-transit SP-1 notification to Turkish VTS. Standard vessels file 24 hours out. Miss the window and you're anchored waiting — at $8,000–$15,000/day port fees.

6+

Multi-authority filing forms

SP-1 pre-arrival notification, crew list, cargo declaration, ballast water management, dangerous goods declaration, and Montreux certification — filed across Turkish VTS, TBSM, and Kıyı Emniyeti. Each has its own format, deadline, and validation rule.

$42K

Average rejection cost

Document errors or late filings result in anchor delays, pilot rebooking fees, and cargo claims. The Bosporus has zero tolerance for SP-1 format errors — Turkish VTS rejects non-compliant submissions without exception.

1936

Montreux Convention restrictions

The 1936 Montreux Convention restricts warships, limits tonnage for non-Black Sea naval vessels, and places conditions on submarine transit. Commercial vessels must still certify compliance. Most operators don't know where to check.

FAQ

Common questions about Bosporus compliance

What documents are required for Bosporus Strait transit?
Turkish maritime authorities (TBSM/Kıyı Emniyeti) require: SP-1 pre-arrival notification (24h for most vessels, 48h for dangerous cargo), crew list, cargo declaration, ballast water management declaration, and Montreux Convention compliance certification for special vessel categories. CanalClear automates all forms and validates against current Turkish Strait Vessel Traffic Services (VTS) requirements.
What is the Montreux Convention and how does it affect Bosporus transit?
The 1936 Montreux Convention governs transit rights through the Turkish Straits. Commercial vessels have freedom of passage in peacetime, but warships face restrictions on tonnage, duration, and prior notice. Certain vessel types (aircraft carriers, submarines under power) are prohibited from transit. CanalClear automatically validates your vessel type against Montreux Convention requirements and flags any compliance issues.
What are the Bosporus transit deadline windows?
Standard vessels must file SP-1 notification 24 hours before expected transit. Vessels carrying dangerous cargo (IMDG classified) must notify 48 hours in advance. CanalClear sends automated alerts at 24hr, 6hr, and 1hr pre-transit milestones, and tracks your filing status against all Turkish VTS requirements.
How many vessels transit the Bosporus each year?
The Bosporus Strait handles approximately 45,000–50,000 vessel transits annually, making it one of the world's busiest and most navigationally challenging international waterways. Combined with the Dardanelles, the Turkish Straits system processes around 55,000 transits per year.
