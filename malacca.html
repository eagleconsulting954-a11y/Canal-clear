The Problem

Three countries, six reporting systems, and a piracy risk zone — the Malacca compliance trap

Every Malacca transit touches Singapore, Malaysia, and Indonesia authorities. Each has its own deadline, format, and validation requirement — and mistakes get flagged across all three simultaneously.

3

Multi-state reporting burden

Singapore's VTIS, Malaysia's VTS Johor and Port Klang, and Indonesia's VTIS Batam all require separate reports. A single transit generates filing obligations across three national maritime authorities — each with its own submission format and deadline window.

300 GT

Mandatory STRAITREP threshold

STRAITREP participation is mandatory for all vessels over 300 GT under IMO Resolution A.858(20). Vessels towing (combined length over 200m) and all vessels carrying dangerous or polluting cargo must also report — regardless of size. Non-participation triggers port state control scrutiny.

1.7nm

Phillips Channel narrows

At its narrowest point near Singapore, the Malacca Strait squeezes to 1.7 nautical miles. The Traffic Separation Scheme (TSS) governs vessel movement through this bottleneck — TSS violations carry port state control sanctions and flag state notifications across the region.

High

Active piracy risk zone

The Strait of Malacca remains on the IMB Piracy Reporting Centre watch list. Under ISPS Code obligations, operators must file anti-piracy risk assessments, maintain BMP compliance documentation, and submit voyage plans to ReCAAP ISC — documentation most operators assemble manually per-transit.

FAQ

Common questions about Strait of Malacca compliance

What documents are required for Strait of Malacca transit?
The Strait of Malacca requires: STRAITREP vessel traffic reporting (mandatory for vessels over 300 GT), Traffic Separation Scheme (TSS) compliance documentation, VTIS Singapore arrival/departure reports, MPA Singapore notices for vessels calling Singapore, anti-piracy risk assessment, and dangerous cargo declarations where applicable. Three Vessel Traffic Information Systems (VTIS) cover the strait — Singapore, Malaysia (VTIS Johor/Port Klang), and Indonesia. CanalClear automates all filings and validates against current MPA and littoral state requirements.
What is STRAITREP and who must comply?
STRAITREP is the mandatory ship reporting system for the Strait of Malacca and Singapore, established under IMO Resolution A.858(20). All vessels of 300 GT and above, vessels towing where the combined length exceeds 200m, and vessels carrying dangerous or polluting cargo must participate. Reports must be filed when entering the reporting zone, when deviating significantly from the filed route, and on exit. CanalClear automates STRAITREP submissions and tracks your vessel against all reporting boundaries.
What are the VTIS reporting deadlines for the Strait of Malacca?
VTIS Singapore requires pre-arrival notice 2 hours before entering Singapore port limits for vessels calling Singapore. STRAITREP positional reports are required at mandatory reporting points throughout the strait. Vessels carrying hazardous cargo must notify the relevant coastal state VTS authority 24 hours in advance. CanalClear tracks all reporting points and sends automated alerts before each boundary crossing.
How many vessels transit the Strait of Malacca each year?
The Strait of Malacca handles approximately 83,000–90,000 vessel transits annually, making it the world's busiest international shipping strait by volume. It carries roughly 30% of global seaborne trade and over 25% of the world's oil supply. At its narrowest point (the Phillips Channel near Singapore), the strait is only 1.7 nautical miles wide.
